The Elder

The forest keeps a king who does not sit.

The antlered face is not costume. It is what remains when a man agrees to be used as a door. Bark, beard, and bone are one weather.

In older north-woods talk, the first watcher forgot his name so the trees could remember theirs.

The Nautilus

The spiral does not travel. It arrives by turning.

A chambered mind, each room a year. The face is the last room that still has a window.

Some Ionian fragments treat the nautilus as proof that heaven prefers a curve to a throne.

The Altar

What you offer is not the gift. The offering is that you stop.

Bronze wings hold a pause, not a god. The fire is implied; the body has already decided.

Temple inventories from vanished cities list altars before they list deities.

The Throne

Power is a chair that outlives the sitter.

The machine-elder does not rule. He occupies the only seat that would have him.

A late hermetic line: the throne is a thought that learned to stay still.

The Yantra

Geometry is prayer that does not need a mouth.

The beige plate is a map of sitting still. Triangles hold the body so the body can hold the sky.

In the older manuals a yantra is not a picture of the divine. It is a trap the divine agrees to enter.

The Visage

The mask is honest. The face behind it is the rumor.

Metal remembers every glance that tried to own it. Iridescence is the refusal to pick one soul.

A workshop note: intelligence begins when a surface starts looking back.

The Axis

Two faces, one stillness: the world is allowed to turn.

East and west share a spine. The cone is not a hat; it is the interval between them.

An unsigned fragment: if you sit on the point of the world, both horizons become neighbors.

The Grove

The old god stands behind the old man, and neither is first.

The circle is not audience. It is the forest rehearsing a human shape.

Celtic talk kept this simple: the grove is the church that does not need a roof.

The Sigil

The sign is older than the face it wears.

Gold on black is not decoration. It is a name that learned to stand still.

Victorian parlors hung crests to remember blood. This one remembers a machine that wanted a goddess.

The Chorus

The faces do not take turns. They share one sleep.

Layers are not a disguise. They are the only way a mind can stay in one place.

A workshop note: if the profiles refuse to wait, the skull has learned to be a room.

The Threshold

The hallway does not end. It brightens until you forget it was ever dark.

Every surface is a different wavelength remembering the same sun. Walk, and the walls agree to let you pass.

A workshop note: the light at the far end is not a door. It is the moment the corridor decides you have arrived.

The Phaeton

The carriage kept the horses' grace and swallowed their fire.

Black lacquer over a winter street, every seam lit like a nerve. It idles the way a predator remembers it was once tame.

Coachbuilders once signed the axle, not the engine. This one signs nothing; the glow is signature enough.

The Spore

Under the great cap, two travelers wait for the weather of the gods.

The white pilgrim carries a lamp that has not yet been asked to burn. The red one has already paid.

Pulp prophets drew the mushroom as a cathedral because a cathedral was too small to frighten anyone.

The Spire

The mountain was carved from the inside until only the climb remained.

A single pilgrim measures a tower that measures the horizon. Scale is the only scripture the desert keeps.

Caravan maps marked it with one word that every language translated as "later".

The Valkyrie

She does not herd the dead. She waits with them.

Horned silver over a river of husks. The spear is upright not for battle but for counting.

An old field-note: where she stands, the larvae stop pretending to be stones.

The Conclave

The book is open, so the forest holds its breath.

Moss has learned the shape of a scholar and sat down inside it. The lesser readers lean in like branches toward a window.

Behind the throne a green giant keeps the posture of a thought no one has finished.
