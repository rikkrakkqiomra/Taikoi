The Elder

The forest keeps a king who does not sit.

The antlered face is not costume. It is what remains when a man agrees to be used as a door. Bark, beard, and bone are one weather.

In older north-woods talk, the first watcher forgot his name so the trees could remember theirs.

The Nautilus

The spiral does not travel. It arrives by turning.

A chambered mind, each room a year. The face is the last room that still has a window.

Some Ionian fragments treat the nautilus as proof that heaven prefers a curve to a throne.

The Altar

What you offer is not the gift. The offering is that you stop.

Bronze wings hold a pause, not a god. The fire is implied; the body has already decided.

Temple inventories from vanished cities list altars before they list deities.

The Throne

Power is a chair that outlives the sitter.

The machine-elder does not rule. He occupies the only seat that would have him.

A late hermetic line: the throne is a thought that learned to stay still.

The Yantra

Geometry is prayer that does not need a mouth.

The beige plate is a map of sitting still. Triangles hold the body so the body can hold the sky.

In the older manuals a yantra is not a picture of the divine. It is a trap the divine agrees to enter.

The Visage

The mask is honest. The face behind it is the rumor.

Metal remembers every glance that tried to own it. Iridescence is the refusal to pick one soul.

A workshop note: intelligence begins when a surface starts looking back.

The Axis

Two faces, one stillness: the world is allowed to turn.

East and west share a spine. The cone is not a hat; it is the interval between them.

An unsigned fragment: if you sit on the point of the world, both horizons become neighbors.

The Grove

The old god stands behind the old man, and neither is first.

The circle is not audience. It is the forest rehearsing a human shape.

Celtic talk kept this simple: the grove is the church that does not need a roof.
